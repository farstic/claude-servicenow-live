# RFP intake card — template

Fill in this card in the first three to five days of a bid, before any drafting. It is the input to gate G1 (bid baseline) and to the go / no-go view. It is internal.

| Item | Value |

| Release family proposed / the customer's current release | |
| Incumbency or proof-of-concept facts | What is already built or known, and which claims depend on it |

## 4. Evaluation model

- **Published weights:** criterion, weight and sub-criteria. Inspect hidden sheets in the customer's templates: weights are often there.
- **No published weights:** derive an implicit model from:
  - the pitch objectives;
  - the minimum contents of the pack;
  - the partner expectations;
  - the accountability statements;
  - the rejection clause.

  Map each criterion to the section that wins its points.

## 5. Scope summary

- **Objectives.** The customer's objectives and its own primary KPIs (objectives → capabilities → KPI map, if given).
- **Capabilities.** Capability areas and sub-areas, with the count of requirement rows.
- **Users and volumes.** Personas and levels; volumes (users, fulfillers, cases, customers, sites, countries).
- **Segmentation.** Dimensions such as customer type, tier, product and region.
- **Phasing.** Phasing expectations (minimum viable product first, waves, roll-out) and the lifecycle journeys.

## 6. Technical scope

- Entity or data model.
- Source systems, with a note of whether each integration already exists.
- Interface matrix: system, direction, method, frequency.
- Channels.
- Architecture principles, for example a preferred integration platform.
- Control areas: access, data protection, AI governance, audit.
- Non-functional requirements and the stated volumes.

## 7. Commercial

- **Pricing model:** fixed price, time and materials, capped, gates or milestones with payment per gate, retention.
- **Flexibility mechanisms:** scope swaps, change control.
- **Required outputs:**
  - rate card;
  - rough order of magnitude;
  - innovative models (risk and reward, gain share);
  - items to price separately;
  - currency and taxes.
- **Contract terms:** warranty, hypercare, validity, payment terms, liability, penalties.

## 8. Legal and process

- Confidentiality.
- Rejection rights.
- Sub-contractor disclosure.
- No-commitment clause.
- Who bears bid costs.
- Binding-proposal rule: whether assumptions must sit inside the response.

## 9. Explicit asks

List every phrase of the form:
- "vendor must", "shall", "required in the response";
- "complete, validate and price";
- "describe", "provide".

Give each one an ID and the artefact asked for. **This list gates submission:** every explicit ask gets its own answer block.

| ID | Ask (customer's words) | Artefact requested | Where answered | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 10. Image transcription log

Requirements often sit only in images: journeys, KPI maps, architecture, governance, gate charts. Transcribe them.

| Image (page, figure) | Requirement content | Requirement IDs created |
|---|---|---|

## 11. Missing inputs and first questions

- **Inputs the RFP refers to but did not include.** Examples: an inventory, appendix "xx", guidelines.
- **Customer-document defects to raise as clarifications.** Examples:
  - placeholders;
  - numbering gaps;
  - RACI rows with no R or A;
  - text carried over from another tender;
  - locked answer columns.
- **First clarification questions,** each with a proposed default. Questions never go on slides.
